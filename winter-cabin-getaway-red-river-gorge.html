RED RIVER GORGE TRIP GUIDE

A winter cabin getaway in Red River Gorge

Quieter trails, bare-tree views and a hot tub waiting at the end of the day.

Why winter is underrated in the Gorge

Most people visit Red River Gorge in fall for the color, which means busy trailheads and booked-up cabins. Winter is the opposite. With the leaves down, you can see the sandstone cliffs and arches from the trail, the parking lots have room, and you often have an overlook to yourself.

Cold snaps can also turn the small waterfalls and seeps along the rock walls into ice, which makes for great photos.

Hikes that work well in winter

Shorter, well-traveled trails are the best bet when it is cold. Sky Bridge is a short loop that crosses the top of an arch. Auxier Ridge rewards you with long views of Courthouse Rock, and Gray’s Arch is a classic half-day hike. Natural Bridge State Resort Park, just down the road from Slade, has trails up to its namesake arch.

Rock and leaves stay slick in winter, especially near cliff edges, so wear boots with real tread, start early and plan to be back before dark. Check the Daniel Boone National Forest and state park pages for closures before you go.

Getting around

The roads into the Gorge are narrow and winding, and shaded curves can stay icy after the main roads are clear. Drive slowly, and keep an eye on the forecast if snow is expected. Cell service is spotty in places, so download maps ahead of time.

Make the cabin the main event

Winter is the season for staying in. All three of our Gorge cabins have a private hot tub, a fire pit and a fully stocked kitchen, so you can hike in the morning, soak in the afternoon and cook a big dinner without going back out. Bootlegger’s Retreat and Trinity have game rooms for long evenings, and all three are pet friendly and have EV chargers.
